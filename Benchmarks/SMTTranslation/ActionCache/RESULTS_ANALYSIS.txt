What this changes in practice
============================

This is a useful opt-in frontend optimization, with a much smaller improvement
in the number of bounded checks solved. All 279 matched completed normalization
measurements were faster: median speedup 25.39x, with a 21.11x reduction in raw
allocation-counter deltas. Complete query production was faster in 132/134
matched completed cases, at a median 1.58x speedup; its measured allocations
fell by a median factor of 1.70. Once cold setup and VC elaboration are included,
the median ratio is only 1.03x, with 40/134 cases slower. These are single primary
observations per cell, not estimates of statistical significance. The cache is
most attractive when a specification is reused for several depths or traces.

The benchmark emits six additional queries, losing none: Blockchain, Chord,
MultiSigAll, MultiSigMajority and SuzukiKasami at depth 32, plus NOPaxos at depth
16. Four are accepted by cvc5. The new Blockchain depth-32 query returns UNSAT:
12.536 seconds producing SMT, 2.590 seconds in the solver, plus 0.316 seconds VC
elaboration and 15.898 seconds cold setup. Two extra solver replays also return
UNSAT in 2.926 and 2.979 seconds. NOPaxos and the two MultiSig queries time out
at 10 seconds; Chord and SuzukiKasami encounter the same solver-rejection class
seen in their shallower emitted queries. Thus emitted queries rise 134 to 140,
but native acceptance rises 124 to 128 and definite answers only 84 to 86.

The primary definite-answer changes are not uniformly beneficial. PaxosFirstOrder
at depth 4 improves from timeout to UNSAT (3.542 seconds); its cached repeats
also solve, while the uncached 30-second control takes 22.428 seconds. Traffic
at depth 32 improves from timeout to SAT (6.519 seconds), but its uncached
repeats straddle the 10-second boundary: one times out and one solves in 9.551
seconds, with a 30-second control solving in 10.710 seconds. Conversely,
Blockchain depth 16 regresses from UNSAT in 0.929 seconds to a 10-second timeout;
that regression repeats, and the cached 30-second control needs 17.351 seconds.
Equivalent query presentation can affect solver search. The experiment does not
isolate which naming, ordering or normalization detail causes each search change.
No SAT/UNSAT contradiction was observed. Solver hardness need not increase
monotonically with exact trace length, as Blockchain depths 16 and 32 illustrate.

Setup costs matter. The median added setup is 0.355 seconds across the 47 pairs
where both setups finish. MultiPaxos increases from 12.903 to 22.466 seconds;
no query is emitted for either variant. Raft's uncached setup takes 56.031 seconds
and cached setup exceeds the 60-second limit; uncached queries also fail later.
NOPaxos in the TLA directory exceeds that setup limit in both variants. Those
timeouts are censored measurements, not zero setup cost. A cache assembled from
normalized individual actions, or selective template generation, could reduce
the eager cost; lazy generation alone would simply move it to the first query.

The query cells have no simp maxSteps failures in this run, but simp-related
problems remain. Cached NOPaxos depth 32, VerticalPaxosFirstOrder depth 32 and
TLA Paxos depth 32 exhaust the overall heartbeat budget in smtSimp. More broadly,
frontend heartbeat failures fall from 20 to seven, while recursion failures
remain at 46 and type-mismatch failures at 45 per variant. First failure phases
can shift when an earlier bottleneck is removed; these counts are not separate
causal effects. Cached runs still stop 91 times in embedding and 44 times in
translation. Better normalization cannot repair those independent backend defects.

The semantic evidence is stronger than equal outcome counts: cache entries are
closed, kernel-checked equality theorems, and all 134 paired emitted formulas
match in the independent conservative logical audit. Of those, 124 pairs are
accepted by native cvc5; all 83 paired definite answers agree. Regression checks
exercise dependent arguments, independent function witnesses, shared intermediate
states, trace decoding and untrusted proof reconstruction. The option remains
experimental and off by default because setup and solver tradeoffs are real.
For supported-query performance, the next target is the remaining smtSimp and
context-processing work. For broader corpus coverage, prioritize the embedding
and translation failures. NEXT_FIXES.txt spells out those experiments and the
semantic constraints they must preserve.
