# Source findings for Veil modularity

The Veil baseline is freshly fetched `origin/main`, commit `eb458cb602d3cf9a19d8f5d8af21edcf27775c8e`. Ivy is `kenmcmil/ivy`, commit `8858a0241ed37141757b7a051acc24c58e49c2f3`, inspected from a shallow checkout. The paper is [Modularity for Decidability of Deductive Verification with Applications to Distributed Systems](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~padon/modularity-for-decidability.pdf), PLDI 2018. These findings describe the inspected baselines; the accompanying implementation and guide add the selected Veil features.

## Selected surface design

The [current design](README.md) uses top-level `veil module` definitions, explicit isolate instantiation, action/procedure, `require`/`ensure` and isolate/dependency proof contexts. There is no state export or observation declaration, no separate component check command, and no mandatory specification block. Reachability export is included in `#gen_theorems`. The facts below distinguish the inspected current-main and thesis implementations from the selected surface.

## Additional Veil implementation on the thesis branch

The initial main-branch findings below do not describe all historical Veil work. A subsequent review of `george-phd-thesis` at `25ff4ef866bdeaa893e9fb310a05f34759403aaa` found working invset proof-context machinery and reachable-invariant theorem generation. See [the detailed thesis findings](thesis-findings.md) and [complete DSL example](../../Modularity-Guide.md). The immediate recommendation is to port and harden those existing features; component ownership and rely/guarantee linking remain new.

## What the paper establishes

The paper uses a simplified language called MDL; its syntax and proof rules are illustrative rather than a literal reference for Ivy. Its central benefit is decomposing verification into different logical fragments. Section 4.2 places invariants at exported boundaries, without granting them at arbitrary internal procedure entries. Section 4.3 gives call and layering rules with transitive interference checks and a restriction on callbacks. Sections 4.4 and 4.5 cover ghost slicing and theory abstraction; ghost removal has invisibility, conservative initialization and termination conditions. These are safety and partial-correctness results, not a modular LTL or real-time composition theorem. See printed pages 668–671, especially Definition 4.7 and Theorems 4.8, 4.10 and 4.11. [Paper](https://www.wisdom.weizmann.ac.il/~padon/modularity-for-decidability.pdf#page=8).

## Review qualifications

The independent Opus proposal contains useful source observations alongside broader claims that need qualification. The `outParam` indices make automatic instance selection problematic; they do not prohibit passing several explicit lens values. Standard Lean elimination can expose a bundled conjunction, so generating reduced invariant definitions is one possible fast-path implementation rather than the only sound route. Rejected recursion and loop syntax do not establish termination of arbitrary opaque Lean computations accepted by an action. An event-first interface still needs representation/refinement evidence to substitute an implementation, even if that evidence is packaged as guarantee and wiring proofs rather than a synchronous call refinement.

Opus also identified two suspicious source patterns: `ModSets.modset` checks a loop variable after its loop, and an export-coverage branch refers to an earlier `actname`. These patterns are present at [ivy_actions.py:1837](https://github.com/kenmcmil/ivy/blob/8858a0241ed37141757b7a051acc24c58e49c2f3/ivy/ivy_actions.py#L1837) and [ivy_isolate.py:1994](https://github.com/kenmcmil/ivy/blob/8858a0241ed37141757b7a051acc24c58e49c2f3/ivy/ivy_isolate.py#L1994). Their reachability and semantic consequences were not established with reproductions in this design task. Treat them as audit leads, not demonstrated soundness bugs or reasons to port the checks unchanged.
